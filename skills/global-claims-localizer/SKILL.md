# Global claims localizer for supplements

The same sentence can be legal in one market and illegal in the next. US brands entering Europe over-claim; European brands entering the US under-sell and miss mandatory steps. Take one source text and produce a compliant version per target market, plus the gates the brand must clear before selling.

Never present this as legal advice. Rules and lists change: tell the user which official source to confirm each claim against, and recommend a regulatory professional before launch and for packaging.

## Step 1: Collect inputs

- Source text (ad, PDP, Amazon bullets, email, packaging) and its source market.
- Target markets and channels (own site, Amazon, TikTok Shop, Meta, Google, retail).
- Full formula with amounts per daily dose.
- Evidence available: studies on the finished product or on each ingredient (dose, duration, population, design).

## Step 2: Extract and classify every claim

For each claim note: the ingredient it relies on, the body function or outcome, any number or timeline, any implied disease or condition. Classify as disease/drug claim, health or function claim, nutrient content claim, generic benefit, or non-health (taste, origin, quality).

A disease or drug claim (treats, cures, prevents, heals, a named condition) is not allowed for a supplement in any of these markets. Remove it everywhere.

## Step 3: Apply each market's model

**United States (FDA + FTC)**
- Structure/function claims are allowed without pre-approval when truthful and not misleading ("supports", "maintains", "promotes" a normal function).
- Label and labeling with a structure/function claim carry the FDA disclaimer: "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease." ("These statements have..." for several claims).
- The company named on the label notifies FDA within 30 days of first marketing a product with the claim.
- FTC applies to all advertising, influencer posts and marketplace pages: every claim needs competent and reliable scientific evidence matching the claim, product, dose and population (FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance). Fake or AI-generated reviews and undisclosed endorsements are prohibited.
- Check new dietary ingredient (NDI) status for novel ingredients and California Prop 65 warnings when relevant.

**European Union**
- Positive list: only authorized health claims from the EU Register of nutrition and health claims (Reg. 1924/2006, 432/2012), with their conditions of use. The claim attaches to the nutrient present at a significant amount, not to the hero ingredient. Many ingredients (collagen, hyaluronic acid, most botanicals) have no authorized claim.
- Supplements are notified per member state (e.g. France, Germany) before sale; check national ingredient rules, especially for botanicals.

**United Kingdom (Great Britain)**
- GB Nutrition and Health Claims Register, close to the EU list but managed separately; verify each claim in the GB register, not only the EU one.

**Switzerland**
- OIDAl annex 14 health claims (close to EU), annex 13 nutrition claims; OCAl for supplement rules. Unlisted claims need prior OSAV/BLV authorization.

**Canada**
- Most supplements are natural health products: a product licence (NPN) from Health Canada is required before sale, and marketing claims must stay within the licensed recommended use. A US structure/function claim cannot simply be reused.

**Australia**
- Most supplements are listed complementary medicines on the ARTG (AUST L): only permitted indications from the TGA list may be used, and advertising must follow the Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code.

## Step 4: Platform gates (verify current policies)

- **Amazon:** supplement listings must match the label, avoid disease claims, and show third-party verification through an Amazon-approved testing, inspection and certification provider where required.
- **Meta:** no assertions about the viewer's personal health or appearance, restrictions on before/after and weight content.
- **Google Ads:** healthcare and unapproved-substances policies; some ingredients and claims are restricted or banned.
- **TikTok Shop:** supplements usually need category approval and documentation.

## Step 5: Output

1. A table with one row per claim: original, then one column per target market with verdict (OK / adapt / remove / needs evidence / needs licence) and the localized wording.
2. The full localized text per market, in the market's language when asked, keeping the brand voice.
3. Market-entry gates per market: registrations, notifications, label changes, disclaimers, platform approvals.
4. Evidence gaps: which claims need which kind of proof, and the official source to confirm each one (FDA, FTC, EU Register, GB register, fedlex annex 14, Health Canada LNHPD, TGA permitted indications).
