# Problem to product

Most supplement brands start from a trending ingredient and look for a problem. The products that last start from a real problem, pick only ingredients with human evidence at an effective dose, and say only what the law allows. Run the five stages in order; each one feeds the next. Show each stage's output before moving on when the user is present.

Ground rules for the whole run:
- Research live. Use web search and open the sources for every efficacy, dose and safety statement; cite meta-analyses, systematic reviews and RCTs in humans first. If a fact cannot be verified, write "unverified" next to it rather than filling the gap.
- A supplement supports normal function or a normal life stage; it never treats a disease. If the problem is a disease (diagnosed depression, osteoporosis, alopecia areata, IBS...), say so, recommend medical care, and only design for the non-disease part of the need, if one exists.
- This is product development support, not medical advice for an individual.

## Stage 1: Problem map

Define the need precisely before any ingredient:
- **Who:** life stage, age range, sex, context (e.g. perimenopausal women 45-55 still working full time).
- **Symptoms or goals ranked by how much they bother people,** using real voice-of-customer sources: reviews of competing products, Reddit and forums, Q&A, search queries. Quote short phrases in the customers' words.
- **What they already try** (diet, HRT, competitors, apps) and **why it disappoints** (side effects, no effect, price, too many pills, stigma).
- **Disease line:** which parts are normal life-stage or function (allowed territory) and which are disease (out of scope).
- **The job to be done** in one sentence.

Output: a problem map table (symptom/goal, priority, customer words, current solutions, frustration) and the chosen 1-3 target outcomes.

## Stage 2: Ingredient evidence

For each target outcome, list candidate ingredients and grade them:

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A | Consistent benefit in meta-analyses or several good RCTs in the target population |
| B | Some positive RCTs, mixed or small effects, or different population |
| C | Mechanistic, animal or single small study only |
| X | Evidence of no effect, or a safety concern that outweighs it |

For every A or B candidate record: effective dose and form used in the trials, time to effect, effect size in plain words, population studied, safety (upper limits, contraindications, drug interactions, populations to exclude), regulatory status per market (EU novel food or botanical rules, US NDI, Swiss lists), cost per daily dose if known, taste/format constraints.

Reject "fairy dusting": an ingredient below its studied dose cannot be marketed for that effect.

Also list the authorized nutrient claims (vitamins, minerals) that fit the outcome: in the EU, UK and Switzerland they are often the only legal way to talk about the benefit.

## Stage 3: Formula brief

- Hero ingredient (grade A, studied dose) plus 1-3 supporting ingredients that add a different benefit or an authorized claim; no more than the format allows at effective doses.
- Format (capsule, powder, gummy, shot) chosen for dose size, taste, routine and margin.
- Daily dose, cure length based on the trials' time to effect, and the reason for each.
- Interactions inside the formula and with common medications for this population; warnings to print.
- A one-line note per ingredient for the manufacturer brief (form, standardization, source).

## Stage 4: Claims per market

Translate the evidence into what can legally be said, per target market (use the global-claims-localizer and claims-check skills if available):
- **US:** structure/function claims with substantiation and the FDA disclaimer; claims about common, mild symptoms of natural life stages can be allowed, wording that implies treating a disease is not.
- **EU, UK, Switzerland:** authorized claims only, attached to nutrients present at a significant amount; ingredients without authorized claims are described factually (name, dose, origin).
Show the gap between the science and the legal claims; it shapes positioning.

## Stage 5: Product concept and launch test

- Positioning statement (for whom, which outcome, why believe, against what alternative).
- 3 name ideas checked for obvious conflicts with existing brands (search), and the reason for each.
- Offer: pack size matching cure length, price per day, subscription, guarantee.
- Product page outline and 3 ad angles that respect platform health policies.
- Cheapest validation before production: landing page with waitlist or pre-order, 5-10 customer interviews, competitor review mining, small paid test on the top angle. Define the success threshold in advance.

## Final output

A product dossier with the five stages in order, a sources list of every study and regulation used, and a short "open questions" list (unverified facts, regulatory checks, costs to quote).
