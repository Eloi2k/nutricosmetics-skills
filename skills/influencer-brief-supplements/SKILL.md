# Influencer brief for supplements

The brand is responsible for what creators say about its supplement. A good brief gives creators freedom on style and zero ambiguity on claims.

## Brief structure

1. **Campaign goal:** awareness, launch, sales (code/link), UGC for ads. One primary goal.
2. **Product and cure basics:** what it is, how to take it, cure length, taste/format, price per day, offer/code.
3. **Creator test period:** ask creators to use the product for a realistic period before posting and to talk about their own experience only.
4. **Key messages (pick 1-2):** ritual and routine, ingredients and origin, why they chose it. Provide the exact authorized claims they may use, word for word.
5. **Do:** show the ritual, texture, preparation, packaging; speak in the first person about experience; mention it is a food supplement; disclose the partnership clearly.
6. **Don't:** promise results or timelines ("erases wrinkles", "in 2 weeks"); medical words (cure, treat, heal, acne, eczema, inflammation); before/after comparisons; comparisons with medicines or aesthetic procedures; claims about pregnancy or medical conditions.
7. **Disclosure:** clear ad/partnership mark at the start (paid partnership label plus #ad / #pub / #werbung depending on market); gifting also requires disclosure in many markets.
8. **Deliverables and timing:** formats, number of posts, posting dates, approval step before posting.
9. **Rights:** whitelisting/Spark Ads/partnership ads, usage duration, channels, raw footage delivery.
10. **Payment and KPIs:** fee, commission, tracking link/code, what will be measured.

## Script review

When reviewing a creator script or video, list every sentence that implies a result or health effect, classify it (OK / reword / remove), and propose a compliant line that keeps the creator's voice. Run claims-check on borderline lines if available.

## Output

Return the brief ready to send (in the creators' language), a one-page do/don't sheet, and a pre-publication checklist.
